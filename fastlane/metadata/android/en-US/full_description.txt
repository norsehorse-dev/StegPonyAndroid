StegPony encrypts a message or a file and hides it inside an ordinary photo, or inside text. The photo still looks and opens like any other. Without the right passphrase or key there is nothing to find: no header, no marker, and no way to tell a photo with something hidden from one without.

No network permission. StegPony can't send anything anywhere, and that is checkable in the manifest. There is no account, no tracking and no ads.

Features:

* Hide text or a file in a JPEG, which stays a JPEG with the camera's own compression, or in a PNG
* Lock it with a passphrase, or with someone's public key so that only their secret key opens it
* Add a decoy: a second message under a second passphrase, to give up if someone makes you open the image. Nobody can tell whether an image has one
* See a detectability rating (low, medium, high) before you hide, based on tests with a standard detector on real phone photos
* Take the cover photo with the in-app camera, so it is never saved anywhere else. StegPony warns when a photo was used before, or looks like a download or a messenger copy
* Hide in text when a photo can't be sent, as a run of ordinary words or as invisible characters inside a message you write
* Reveal with a passphrase, your saved keys, or an OpenPGP hardware key over NFC or USB, where the secret never leaves the key. Tested with a Token2 PIN+, and with a Nitrokey 3A NFC over USB
* Keys are age-compatible. Import age identities and Curve25519 OpenPGP keys from PGPony or GnuPG, and add contacts by QR code, file, paste or share
* App lock, screenshots blocked on sensitive screens, a clipboard that clears after a minute, and an optional launcher disguise
* English, German, Spanish, French, Brazilian Portuguese, Russian and Simplified Chinese, switchable inside the app

How to send what you hide: as a file or document attachment. A messenger's photo mode recompresses the picture and destroys what's hidden.

What StegPony can't protect against: hiding lowers the odds of being noticed, it doesn't make them zero. It can't protect you from someone who has the original photo, from a forensic examination of your unlocked phone, or from anyone who sees whom you send images to and when. StegPony hides what you send, not that you sent it. The Help page in the app covers the limits in full, and is worth reading before relying on it.

Passphrases are stretched with Argon2id, keys use X25519, and the content is sealed with ChaCha20-Poly1305. The format and all the image and crypto work are in StegPonyCore, a separate open-source Rust library.
